# SunD Project Context (Sime Darby Township Hackathon)

Context carried over from a claude.ai planning conversation (Oct 2026). Use this as background for any work on the SunD pitch deck, mockups, or prototype.

## Hackathon theme
**Planet:** How might we create townships that regenerate the environment while making sustainable living effortless for communities, residents and businesses?
Sub-themes touched: Energy efficiency, Green transport, Circular economy, Climate resilience.

## Concept names (final)
- **SunD Wallet:** credits app for residents.
- **SunD Credits (SC):** the wallet's currency. 1 SC = RM 1 to spend at SunD Bays, SunD eBike and green facilities.
- **SunD Bay:** hybrid solar EV charging bay (and future sub-brand).
- **SunD eBike:** solar-charged eBike docks that also accept SunD Credits.
- Casing is always **SunD** (capital S, capital D).

## Core idea in one line
Two engines joined by one wallet:
1. **CREAM engine:** SD controls rooftop solar under CREAM and routes the energy where it is most valuable (nearby commercial areas, data centres). Landed owners are rewarded with SunD Credits plus an optional bill rebate.
2. **SunD Bay engine:** hybrid charging bays powered by their own canopy solar and battery, with grid backup, inside and beyond townships, open to every EV driver at lower prices.

Credits earned from roofs are spent at SunD Bays, so the value loops back into solar.

## 1. Problems we solve
1. **EVs are growing faster than chargers.**
   - 122,286 EVs registered all-time by July 2026.
   - Only 6,416 public chargers as of May 2026 (2,143 DC + 4,273 AC), about 19 EVs per charger.
   - The 10,000-charger target for end-2025 was missed; the new target is 30,000 by 2030.
   - EY survey: 27% of potential buyers are deterred by too few charging points. Their concerns: finding a charger 61%, long waits 53%, charging cost 38%.
2. **Charging runs on fossil power.** Peninsular Malaysia's 2025 generation was 58.6% coal and 33.1% gas, about 92% fossil.
3. **CREAM's cash reward leaks out of the green loop.**
   - Under SD's CREAM scheme (with TNB/GSPARX), homeowners lease their roofs for cash income.
   - That cash is spent elsewhere: fossil-powered chargers, food, shopping.
   - Residents never use or see their own solar.
4. **Shared solar is hard to deliver.**
   - CREAM is limited to a 5 km radius and pays a Community Access Charge of 9 sen/kWh (cut from 15).
   - CRESS (any distance) costs 18 sen/kWh for firm supply (14 + 4, Oct 2026) or 40 sen/kWh for non-firm.
   - Solar oversupplies at midday and undersupplies at night.
   - Panels shared with shops and offices compete for the same energy.
   - Meters, connections and approvals add cost and complexity.

## 2. Target users
| User | Challenge |
|---|---|
| SD landed owners | Their roofs generate solar they never use; they can only charge at home |
| SD condo residents | No home charger space and limited roof area |
| Public EV drivers | Hard to find bays, long waits, expensive DC charging |
| Future EV buyers | Worried about charging access |
| Sime Darby Property | Large solar capacity but no direct link to residents or EV demand |

## 3. Solution

### SunD Wallet
- **Solar income:** the energy SD sells from each roof is valued at the **normal selling rate, RM 0.25/kWh** (benchmark: average system marginal price ~RM 0.20–0.25/kWh). A 10 kWp roof (~1,100 kWh/month) earns about **RM 275/month**.
- **Redemption mix (owner's choice):** each owner sets how that income is redeemed:
  - **SunD Credits:** unlimited share (50–100%), with a **+20% top-up**: RM 1 of income → RM 1.20 in credits. 1 SC = RM 1 at SunD Bays, eBikes and green facilities.
  - **Cash rebate on the electricity bill:** 0–50%, RM for RM.
  - Example at 25% rebate: ~RM 69 off the bill + ~250 SC (≈ 1,700 km of driving) a month.
  - **All three figures (RM 0.25 rate, +20% top-up, 50% cap) are settings SD can customise later**, e.g. to match the actual offtake price, lower the cap as bays mature, or raise it for non-EV owners.
  - Why it works: credits are worth 20% more than cash and the top-up costs SD little (bay solar costs ~12–18 sen/kWh), so SunD usage is encouraged while owners without an EV still benefit.
- **Credits first, cash capped.** Unlimited cash would lead to overconsumption (e.g. extra air-con) and leak out of the loop. The rebate goes to the electricity bill, not free cash.
- **Condo residents** get credits from 50% of their canopy share's income (~RM 37/month) plus 15% off charging.
- Credits are a selling point for non-SD buyers and encourage EV purchases.
- Funded from the CREAM energy SD sells to offtakers, plus public charging profit.

### CREAM energy routing (SD's control)
- Under CREAM, SD aggregates and controls the rooftop energy. It can transmit the surplus within 5 km to the offtakers it prefers, such as SD commercial areas, offices or data centres. This creates revenue for SD and green power for those sites.
- Landed owners still benefit through SunD rewards (credits + bill rebate), and sustainability spreads to the businesses using the energy.
- **CREAM only launched in Sep 2025 and is new for Malaysian landed homes.** That makes it the ideal starting point: SunD can be designed into future SD developments from day one, rather than retrofitted.

### SunD Bay (hybrid)
- Power sources: on-site solar canopy (0 km, primary), battery (evening and night), TNB grid (backup only). **Independent of CREAM**, so a bay can be placed anywhere: inside the township or beyond it.
- **100% of the bay's own panels supply EV owners**, not shared with shops.
- **Modular:** add canopies where demand is high, move them where it's low.
- Locations by demand: SD malls, commercial areas, parks, clubhouses. Later: beyond the township and at petrol stations.
- SunD Wallet users pay with credits (no cash). The public pays at cheaper rates (proposed ~RM 1.00/kWh DC).
- **Solar Surplus Discount:** a bay with high stored solar lowers its price to avoid waste.
- **Saver Credits:** pay RM20 to get RM30 in credits, usable only at high-solar bays.
- **Bonus credits** for charging at SunD instead of non-renewable bays.

### Circular ecosystem
Earn (SD routes CREAM energy → owners get credits + rebate) → Charge at SunD Bays (credits redeem there) → Reuse (bay canopy + battery, no grid transmission) → Expand (energy sales + charging revenue fund more bays, in Elmina and beyond).

Slogans:
- "CREAM pays cash that leaves the loop. SunD pays credits that come back to the sun."
- "SunD: Your roof earns it. Your car drives it. Your township lives it."

## 4. Innovation vs existing approaches
| Existing | SunD |
|---|---|
| CREAM pays cash, which leaks out | Owner chooses credits (unlimited) + bill rebate (≤50%); value loops back into solar |
| Shared solar is limited to 5 km and pays grid charges | On-site solar, 0 km, no transmission charge or loss |
| Panel output shared with shops | 100% of bay panels go to EVs |
| Midday solar is wasted | Battery plus Solar Surplus Discount |
| Grid-powered bays (~92% fossil) | Hybrid, solar first |
| Fixed infrastructure | Modular, demand-based |
| Township-only perks | Open to every EV driver; brand extends beyond SD |

## 5. Impact (every side wins)
- **Public:** cheaper charging (~RM 1.00 DC vs RM 1.20–1.80; surplus price ~RM 0.80; Saver Credits +50%), more bays. Sustainable charging for everyone, not only SD residents.
- **SD owners:** solar income as credits (+20%) or a bill rebate, charging without cash, beyond home.
- **SD:** new revenue stream, more control over its solar, a property selling point, and support for its ESG targets (Scope 1 and 2 emissions down 40% by 2030; net zero by 2050).
- **Planet:** fossil charging replaced with solar.

## 5b. Why demand risk is very low
1. **Built-in demand:** SD credit holders can only spend credits at SunD, so every bay starts with users (450 Elmina homes ≈ RM 125k solar income/month, of which at least half, ≈ 75k–150k SC, must be spent at SunD).
2. **Cheapest solar charging nearby:** prices below the main networks attract the public.
3. **Saver Credits:** pre-sold, so SD gets cash upfront before the energy is used.
4. **Modular:** an under-used canopy can be moved to a busier site.
5. **Many uses:** cars, eBikes and the clubhouse all draw on the same credits.

## 5c. Long-term potential (not limited to the township)
- SunD Bays at other SD townships, malls, offices, campuses, highway stops and partner petrol stations, each with its own canopy and battery outside the 5 km CREAM zone.
- SunD eBike, fleets (delivery, e-hailing, shuttles) and other mobility.
- Licensing the SunD platform to other developers and councils.
- One SunD Wallet usable across every SunD location.

## 6. Roadmap
| Phase | Timeline | Action |
|---|---|---|

## 7. Key data (estimates unless sourced)

### Sime Darby CREAM facts
- Elmina pilot: 4.5 MW across 450 homes, about 10 kWp (~19 panels) per house.
- Target: lease 6,000 rooftops within 5 km.
- Pilot launched 26 Sept 2025. Power goes to SD's Innovation Park.
- Elmina also has Malaysia's first Community Energy Storage System, and Elmina Ridge homes have their own battery systems.

### Solar yield assumptions
- 1 kWp produces about 110–130 kWh a month (~3.6 kWh a day).
- 1 panel (~550 W, ~2.6 m²) produces about 2 kWh a day.
- 1 parking lot (2.5 m × 5 m = 12.5 m²) fits about 2.5 kWp (~5 panels), producing about 9 kWh a day.

### EV assumptions (Proton e.MAS 5)
- Prime: 30.12 kWh, 225 km. Premium: 40.16 kWh, 325 km.
- AC charging 6.6 kW; DC charging 53 kW (Prime) to ~70 kW (Premium).
- About 6.8 km per kWh after charging losses, so 1 panel ≈ 13.5 km a day.

### Tariff
- TNB domestic (July 2025), up to 1,500 kWh: 27.03 sen energy + 4.55 capacity + 12.85 network, about 44.4 sen/kWh.

- 1 SD roof ≈ 2 trips around the Earth a year.
- 450 Elmina homes ≈ 2.8 trips around the Earth every day.

### SunD Bay sizes, cost, time
| Size | Solar | Chargers | Battery | Est. cost | Setup time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small (10 lots) | 25 kWp | 4 AC | 50 kWh | RM175k–325k | 2–3 months |

Unit costs:
- Carport solar: RM3k–8k per kWp (used RM4k–6k).
- AC charger installed: RM3k–8k.
- DC 60 kW installed: RM60k–80k.
- Commercial battery: RM1,800–2,800 per kWh (used RM1k–2.5k).
- Charger installation takes 2–5 days. A DC charger needs a new TNB meter, which takes 4–12 weeks.
- Solar carports pay back in about 4–6 years; a 60% Green Investment Tax Allowance applies to charger installation.

### Cost per kWh
- On-site canopy solar: ~12–18 sen.
- Transmitted rooftop solar: ~19–21 sen (including 9 sen CAC).
- Grid: ~44 sen.
- Public DC charging: ~RM1.20.

| | Existing bay | SunD Bay |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Grid (~92% fossil) | 25 kWp canopy + 50 kWh battery + grid |
| Solar share | 0% | ~40% |
| Cars and mileage per day | ~8 cars, ~1,400 km | Same (~600 km of it solar) |
| Price (DC / AC) | RM1.20–1.80 / RM0.75–1.15 | ~RM1.00 / ~RM0.70 (proposed) |

Honest trade-off: SunD costs more upfront. It matches an existing bay's daily margin with about 35% more usage, which SD credit users provide.
